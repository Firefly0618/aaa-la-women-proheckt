The Gilded Age

Financial independence can give women more freedom because it does allow them to earn their own money in a unique way, and they can support themselves, and make their own decisions. An example like during the Gilded Age around (1870-1900), more women began working in factories, offices and textile mills as the country as revolutionized and industrialize. Having jobs allowed women to earn money and make a living out of it which shows their Independence growing instead of relying completely on their families or husbands. However, women were usually paid less than men, had fewer career opportunities, and were still pretty much expected to take care of the home like households, chores, women work, taking care of the family, etc.

Modern Opportunities and Remaining Barriers

Compared to the Gilded Age, women today have many in face basically the same number of opportunities as men have like attending college, work ethics, own business, and pursuing careers. However, some problems do exist on this day. According to the International Labor Organization, around 708 million women worldwide were outside the workforce in 2023 because of some unpaid responsibilities like childcare and caring for their family members. Women also continue to experience unequal pay and fewer opportunities in certain workplaces. The OCED also known as (Organization for Economic Co-Operation and Development) also explains that women generally spend some of their time doing unpaid work at home, which puts a hefty weight on their body also making it harder to focus on their careers.

Connection to "The Awakening"

Let’s talk about how this can connect to “The Awakening” by Kate Chopin. This topic specifically focuses on Women’s financial Independence. They both relate to each other because they both explore women’s struggle for freedom and their Independence during the late 1800’s. In the novel, Edna Pontellier begins to question the expectations society places on women, especially as being wives or housemaids. She wants to have more freedom, to control more of her life instead of depending on her husband to follow society’s rules. She begins pursuing things like painting, reading, etc. This connects to the Gilded Age because women are hoping and seeking for career opportunities and financial freedom, even though they defy society’s rules back then, Both the novel and historical research shows that women wanted more control over their life than society controlling them.

Conclusion & Economic Impact

Overall, Financial Independence can give women more control over their lives and allows them to have a stronger more bravado voice in society. It gives them the freedom to choose their own careers, make financial decisions, and to work towards their desire or personal goals. It can also benefit the economy because this means more women can work for a living also boosting the production rate of certain things overall making the world revolutionize faster. Overall, women have made major progress since the Gilded Age, but there are still barriers that can make it harder for women to achieve equal financial independence.
